# 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 [Creation to the Cross - for Kids](../README.md)

## 🏁 Getting Started

Open the lesson files below to begin teaching or reviewing the material. Each lesson includes a main lesson guide plus supporting resources where available.

## 🧭 [Lesson Plan](./lesson-plan.md) & Overviews

- [Lesson 1: Introducing the Bible](./1/README.md)

  This lesson introduces the Bible to your students. Included are some interesting facts and general information about the Bible and how it came to us. The main point of this lesson is to establish the fact that the Bible is the Word of God.

- [Lesson 2: God Alone](./2/README.md)

  This lesson begins to lay the foundational truth of God's sovereignty by showing that God existed alone before the beginning and that He exists independently of all else and needs nothing. It gives a very brief introduction to the Trinity.

- [Lesson 3: God Created the Angels](./3/README.md)

  This lesson establishes that God created all the spirit beings. It presents God as sovereign and holy, the creator of all created beings, creating all things perfect. It also shows God's sovereignty and holiness in dealing with Lucifer's willful rebellion against God.

- [Lesson 4: God Created the Heavens and the Earth - Day 1 & 2](./4/README.md)

  This lesson is more than a lesson on the biblical facts of days 1 and 2 of creation, when God created light and the sky. God's nature and attributes are clearly displayed in His creative acts. Your primary goal in teaching on creation is to point out the attributes of God through His creative acts.

- [Lesson 5: God Created the Heavens and the Earth - Day 3 & 4](./5/README.md)

  This lesson is more than a lesson on Biblical facts of day 3 & 4 of creation, when God created the dry land, plants, sun, moon, and stars. God's nature and attributes are clearly displayed in His creative acts. Your primary goal in teaching on creation is to point out the attributes of God through His creative acts.

- [Lesson 6: God Created the Heavens and the Earth - Day 5 & 6](./6/README.md)

  This lesson is more than a lesson on Biblical facts of day 5 & 6 of creation, when God created the birds, fish, and land animals. God's nature and attributes are clearly displayed in His creative acts. Your primary goal in teaching on creation is to point out the attributes of God through His creative acts.

- [Lesson 7: Man - God's Special Creation](./7/README.md)

  This lesson presents God as the sovereign Creator and owner of man and presents man as God's special creation, made in the image of God. The lesson emphasizes that God created the earth and everything in it specifically to prepare for His most amazing creation, namely man. Man alone was made in God's image with a mind to know God, emotions to love God, and a will to choose to obey God. Adam, the first and only man created from the ground, the ancestor of all people.

- [Lesson 8: Adam in the Garden](./8/README.md)

  This lesson continues to present God's sovereignty as Creator and owner of man. It shows how God completed His creation and rested, demonstrating that He always finishes what He plans to do. The lesson establishes that God planted a garden for Adam and put him there, and instructed him about two trees in the garden. One gave eternal life and the other would bring the penalty for sin, death, meaning separation from God.

- [Lesson 9: God Made Eve](./9/README.md)

  This lesson shows God's sovereign creation of woman as a gift to man. It emphasizes that God created Eve in the special way He chose, that Eve was made to be Adam's helper and companion, and that marriage is part of God's design for His people.

- [Lesson 10: Adam and Eve Disobeyed](./10/README.md)

  This lesson presents Satan's deception of Eve and Adam's disobedience of God's command. It emphasizes how Satan tricked Eve, how Adam and Eve chose to disobey God, and how their sin separated them from God.

- [Lesson 11: God's Promise and Curse](./11/README.md)

  This lesson tells the Bible story of how Satan deceived Eve and how Adam and Eve chose to disobey God. It shows how their sin separated them from God, while also revealing God's omniscience, holiness, love, and grace as He saw them, called to them, judged the serpent and their sin, and promised to send a Savior.

- [Lesson 12: The Consequences of Sin and the Birth of Cain and Abel](./12/README.md)

  This lesson helps children see God's holiness and grace in the story of Adam and Eve after the fall. It teaches that the coverings Adam and Eve made were not acceptable to God, that God provided clothing of skins for them, and that He sent them out of the garden and guarded its entrance so they could not return. It also shows that God is the giver of life and that Cain and Abel, like all of Adam's descendants, were born outside the garden as sinners, separated from God.

- [Lesson 13: God Rejected Cain's Offering and Accepted Abel's](./13/README.md)

  This lesson presents God's acceptance of Abel, who approached Him by faith in the way God wanted, and His rejection of Cain, who tried to come to God on his own terms.

  God's holiness is shown in His requirement of the death penalty for sin, while His grace and mercy are seen in His provision of a way for sinners to draw near to Him and in His patient reasoning with angry Cain. God judged Cain for murdering his brother, and Cain's descendants are portrayed as worldly-minded. Yet God continued the line of the promised Savior by raising up Seth to replace Abel.

- [Lesson 14: God Judged the World and Saved Noah](./14/README.md)

  This lesson covers the period from Cain and Abel to Noah and the flood. It highlights God's omniscience and wrath against sin, alongside His grace and patience in calling people to repentance. God's sovereignty and omnipotence are displayed in His control over all creation.

  In God's instructions to Noah about building the ark, we see His precise communication and the truth that there is only one way of salvation. The ark serves as a picture of Christ, a theme you will develop further when teaching the New Testament.

  The moral decay of humanity in Noah's day is compared with the decadence of our own society, with a warning against hard-heartedness. God's deliverance of Noah and his family is presented as an act of grace—God's kindness to undeserving sinners. The flood is treated as historical fact, an exercise of God's sovereign power in judging unrepentant sinners.

- [Lesson 15: God Remembered Noah in the Ark and Scattered at Tower of Babel](./15/README.md)

  This lesson wraps up the account of Noah and his family's salvation and their repopulation of the earth after the flood, then moves to the story of the tower of Babel. It highlights God's omniscience and sovereign control over history, especially as seen in how He scattered the rebellious builders and brought about the confusion of languages.

- [Lesson 16: God Called Abram and Lot Chose Sodom and Gomorrah](./16/README.md)

  This lesson highlights God's faithfulness and grace in calling Abram out of Ur and into the land of Canaan, as well as Abram's response of faith. In Genesis 12, God begins to unfold His covenant promises to Abram, declaring that He will make him a great nation and that all the families of the earth will be blessed through his offspring. The lesson also examines Lot's decision to choose what appeared to be the better land (the region of Sodom and Gomorrah), illustrating the contrast between walking by faith and choosing by sight.

- [Lesson 17: God Renewed Promises to Abraham](./17/README.md)

  This lesson explores the foundational biblical truth that God declared Abraham righteous, which was not on the basis of his works, but solely because of his faith. It examines how God communicated directly with Abraham, revealing His covenant promises, which will be referenced and developed in future lessons. Abraham's response serves as a model for all believers, demonstrating that coming to God requires faith rather than human effort or merit.

- [Lesson 18: God Destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah](./18/README.md)

  This lesson shows God's rescue of Lot from Sodom and Gomorrah before He destroyed those wicked cities. It emphasizes that God sees and knows every sin, that He is patient and merciful, and that He cares about people individually. At the same time, it reminds us that God will certainly judge sin.

- [Lesson 19: God Provided a Substitute for Isaac](./19/README.md)

  This lesson shows God's supreme faithfulness, grace, and sovereign power in giving Isaac and then providing a ram to die in Isaac's place. Abraham's faith is presented as a correct response to God's faithfulness. This story is a type of Christ, which you can use in presenting the Gospel.

- [Lesson 20: God Kept His Promise to Abraham Through Isaac and Jacob](./20/README.md)

  This lesson follows the story of Abraham's family from Isaac to Jacob and highlights God's faithfulness in keeping His promise to send a Savior. It shows that Esau did not value God's promises, while Jacob did. God then gives Jacob a dream to reveal His plan to send a Savior who would reconcile people to God.

- [Lesson 21: God Cares for Joseph and brings Israel to Egypt](./21/README.md)

  This lesson presents God's sovereign working and through the life of Joseph to bring Israel into Egypt. God's faithfulness is highlighted through Joseph's trust in Him. God communicated with Joseph through dreams and brought to fulfillment all He had promised.

- [Lesson 22: God Protected and Called Moses](./22/README.md)

  God's sovereign, loving care is shown as He preserves Israel in slavery and raises up Moses to deliver them. The lesson highlights God's omniscience in knowing Pharaoh's response and Satan's counter-moves to thwart the coming Savior lineage. It also shows humanity's inability to save itself, as Moses attempts to save Israel his their own and fails. God's holiness is seen in the burning bush encounter, where He commissions Moses by declaring, “I AM” sent you.

- [Lesson 23: God Sent Plagues on Egypt; God Passed Over Israel](./23/README.md)

  This lesson presents God's sovereignty, omniscience, and omnipotence in brining the plagues on Egypt. It shows His holiness, grace, and mercy in His saving Israel through the Passover. We will see God's sovereignty, omniscience, and omnipotence when God uses Pharaoh's resistance to display Gods power and save Israel. We will see God's faithfulness when He preserved Israel and the family line for the Savior. The Israelites, like us, were unable to save themselves and needed God to make a way to save them. We will finally learn details of The Passover, a type of Jesus Christ, which will be recalled later in presenting the Gospel.

- [Lesson 24: God Saved Israel at the Red Sea](./24/README.md)

  This lesson presents God's power, faithfulness, grace, and mercy as shown in His provision for His often unfaithful people, Israel. It stresses the inability of the Israelites to save themselves. God helped Israel because of His promises to Abraham, because He was going to give His message to the world through Israel, to display His power, so they and we would believe, and because He loved them.

- [Lesson 25: God Provided For Israel in the Desert](./25/README.md)

  This lesson continues to reveal God's power, faithfulness, grace, and mercy through His provision for His often-unfaithful people, Israel. Despite their grumbling and lack of trust, God continually cared for and sustained them throughout their wilderness journey. He helped Israel because of His promises to Abraham, because He intended to deliver His message to the world through them, because He desired to display His power so that they and we would believe, and because He loved them.

- [Lesson 26: Preparation for the Ten Commandments](./26/README.md)

  This lesson sets the stage for the giving of the Ten Commandments. It emphasizes God's righteousness and holiness, as well as His fearful wrath and certain judgment against all sin. God's covenant with Israel depended on the people's obedience, yet they did not understand that they were incapable of obeying Him perfectly. The boundaries established around Mount Sinai for both the people and the animals emphasize God's holiness, humanity's sinfulness, and the certainty of judgment for anyone who approaches God in sin. The Ten Commandments demonstrate that God requires perfect obedience from every person. Although the Law does not reveal the way of grace, it serves an important purpose: it shows us that salvation cannot be achieved through our own good works. In this way, God prepares sinners to receive the gift of salvation made possible through Christ's sacrificial death on their behalf.

- [Lesson 27: God Gave the Ten Commandments](./27/README.md)

  God's sovereignty is emphasized throughout this lesson. Because each commandment was given by Him, it represents His righteous standard. The lesson contrasts God's holiness with humanity's sinfulness and inability to keep His perfect Law. Each commandment is read, explained, and applied to everyday life, showing that everyone has broken God's Law. The purpose of studying the Ten Commandments is to bring conviction of sin, help students recognize that they cannot save themselves through good works, and reveal their need for a Savior.

- [Lesson 28: The Tabernacle](./28/README.md)

  This lesson outlines the basic plan of the tabernacle, focusing only on elements that will help the student understand the Gospel in later lessons. Mercy is defined here as God's provision for sinners to escape the punishment they deserve for their sins. The lesson emphasizes that people can approach God only according to His will and plan.

- [Lesson 29: Israel's Unbelief](./29/README.md)

  This lesson presents God's judgment, grace, mercy, and faithfulness toward unfaithful Israel. These truths are revealed through the report of the men who spied out the Promised Land and the people's continued unbelief. The account of the bronze serpent is also presented as a type of Christ and a means of illustrating the Gospel.

  The lesson emphasizes that humanity is sinful, dependent on God, and helpless to save itself. Therefore, each person must come to God by faith. As in every lesson, the focus remains on God, His character, and His actions.

- [Lesson 30: Israel in the Promised Land Under Judges and Kings](./30/README.md)

  This lesson covers the period from Joshua through the judges and the kings of Israel, offering only a brief overview that highlights David, his plan for the temple, and the temple's completion under Solomon.

  Throughout, the emphasis remains on God's judgment, mercy, grace, and faithfulness to His people Israel, as well as His promise to send a Savior for the whole world.
