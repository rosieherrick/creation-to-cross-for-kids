## 🧭 Lesson 20: God Kept His Promise to Abraham Through Isaac and Jacob

### 📖 Scripture – Genesis 25:19-21, 24-27; 28:10-15; 29:1

**Additional Scriptures for Study:** Genesis 35:1, 35:6, 35:9; John 14:6; John 1:51; 1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 11:9; 2 Corinthians 5:19, 5:21; Psalm 33:11.

**Scriptural References for Jacob and Esau:** Genesis 25:19-34; 27:1-33:20; 35:1-29; Romans 9:10–13; Hebrews 11:20-21, 12:15–17

### 🎯 Lesson Goals

- To show the faithfulness of God to carry out His promise to bring a Savior.
- To show that God works through ordinary, sinful men to bring about His desired results.
- To show that there is only one way to God and that He alone provides that way.

### 💡 This Lesson Should Help the Students

- To see God's faithfulness.
- To see the folly of choosing a worldly lifestyle.
- To realize that they are helpless to save themselves from the penalty of sin.
- To see that God is the only One who can make a way to save them from the penalty of their sins.

### 🧭 Overview

This lesson follows the story of Abraham's family from Isaac to Jacob and highlights God's faithfulness in keeping His promise to send a Savior. It shows that Esau did not value God's promises, while Jacob did. God then gives Jacob a dream to reveal His plan to send a Savior who would reconcile people to God.

### ✨🔑 Key Verse – Jeremiah 32:27 NIV

> I am the LORD, the God of all mankind. Is anything too hard for Me?

### 💭 Perspective for the Teacher

Psalm 146:5 says, _“Happy is he that hath the God of Jacob for his help, whose hope is in the LORD his God.”_ The God of Jacob? When we read the Bible's complete account of Jacob, we see that Jacob was now always a man of faith. Yet Jacob the schemer became Jacob the man of faith. He is listed in the “hall of faith” in Hebrews 11:9, 20, 21, and he is the man whom God chose to be the father of the twelve tribes which became the nation Israel.

As we who teach read for ourselves the whole story of Jacob, it may serve to remind us that God's ways are not man's ways. We might never have “chosen” a man like Jacob. In the same way, some of our students may not be particularly easy to teach; they may be, in fact, downright difficult! But we must remember that God knows the end from the beginning, and He has brought these students to us so that we can give them His Word.

How do we know? The student who seems the least likely to believe may astonish us with his response to God's Word! One of the beautiful things about the story of Jacob is that God spoke personally to Jacob several times and gave His promises to him.

May we be faithful to each in the power of God's Spirit so that we may love the unlovely and share the Word with complete faith in the One who can transform even the vilest sinner into His own dear child! The “Jacob” that plagues us may turn to be the “father” of the great “tribe” of believers!
