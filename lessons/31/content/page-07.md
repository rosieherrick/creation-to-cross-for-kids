# 🧭 [Lesson 31: Israel Refused to Respond to God's Prophets](../README.md)

## 🧩 Synagogues Built For Teaching the Law

During this time, the Jews built meeting houses called **synagogues**, where they could read and teach the Old Testament Scriptures.

- Because of persecution and captivity, the Jewish people were scattered to many countries.
- Synagogues were built, not only in most of the cities and towns in Israel, but also in many cities in Asia Minor, Greece, Persia, and North Africa.
- On the last day of the week, the Jews met in their synagogues, and the Jewish religious teachers and leaders read and explained the writings of Moses and the prophets, that is, the Old Testament.
- But in many cases, their explanations were not according to what God had spoken and written in the Bible.
- The majority of the Jews went to the temple and the synagogues, but they did not truly trust in the Lord or obey His Word.
- They were meeting because it was their habit and they felt it was their duty; they were not worshiping God from the heart.

## 🙏 Some Jews Believed God

🧵Theme - Man must have faith in order to please God and be saved.

🧵Theme - Man can come to God only according to God's will and plan.

However, there were always a small number who believed God's Word given through His prophets.

- They trusted in the Lord and tried to follow His commands.
- They were accepted by the Lord because of their **faith** in Him.
- They were waiting for the coming Savior whom God had promised would save them from Satan and the punishment for their sins.
- Thousands of years had passed since God had first promised to send the Savior, but they knew that He would come at the exact time God had planned.

## 🧩 Conclusion

Israel's unbelief and rejection of God's promises made God very sad.

But even though Israel made God sad, He never changed His plans or forgot His promises.

Now He was going to show His grace and mercy. He wasn't just showing it to Israel, but to the whole world!

God had established a common language, Greek, and excellent road system built by the Romans, and Jewish synagogues (meeting places) in distant countries.

He was making a way for **everyone** to hear about the long-promised Savior.
